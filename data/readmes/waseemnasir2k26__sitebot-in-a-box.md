**The prospect says "show me a chatbot on my content" and your demo is a generic bot that makes things up. This crawls their site and gives you a live widget that answers from their pages, links the source, and says "I don't know" when the site has no answer.**

```bash
git clone https://github.com/waseemnasir2k26/sitebot-in-a-box.git && cd sitebot-in-a-box && pip install -e . && python -m sitebot demo
```

That runs the whole pipeline offline on a bundled fixture site: no keys, no downloads. This is its real output (generated by `scripts/gen_readme_demo.py` with the built-in hashed embeddings, the same as `python -m sitebot demo --embedder hash`, which is the default; a test fails if it drifts; the port is random, shown as PORT):

## How it answers

Every question ends in one of five answers. The first four say what the site covers, and a correction says what it covers instead; the last one says it does not know.

| Kind          | When                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Example (offline mock, fixture site, hashed embeddings)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | `grounded`  |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------- |
| **Full**      | ONE sentence on the pages covers every required word of the question (its section heading counts as context). Function words, light verbs, the trade word "bike", chat filler and the trigger words of an intent the sentence matches are not required on their own. | `where r u located m8` → `Visit us at 12 Harbor Street. [1]`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | `true`      |
| **Partial**   | Every required word is on the pages, but no single sentence has them all, or a word is not on the pages. The answer names what the pages do not say together, or the missing word, then quotes only the sentences it uses.                                           | `is the puncture repair warranty 6 months?` → `The site doesn't mention 6 months for that. Here's what it says about warranty: We also sell refurbished commuter bikes with a 6 month warranty. [1] A puncture repair costs $12 per wheel, or $18 with a new inner tube fitted. [2]` | `"partial"` |
| **Correction** | A number in the question (a price, a time, a duration or a count) is not the number the pages give for the same kind, and one sentence on the pages covers the rest of the question. The answer quotes that sentence and gives its number. Never sent to the model. | `is the tune-up $35?` → `The site says $60, not $35: A basic tune-up costs $60 and covers brake and gear adjustment, tyre pressure and a chain clean. [1]` | `"correction"` |
| **Soft miss** | An intent was asked, but the main subject of the question is not on the pages, so no sentence answers it. The visitor gets the two most relevant pages to look at.                                                                                                   | `how much is a zorbulator` → `I couldn't find that exactly. These pages may help: Services and prices \| Lumen Bike Repair (…/services/), Opening hours \| Lumen Bike Repair (…/hours.html)`                                                                                                           | `false`     |
| **Refuse**    | No intent, and a main subject of the question is not on the pages.                                                                                                                                                                                                   | `do you sell drones?` → `I don't know, contact hello@lumenbikes.example`                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | `false`     |

Real outputs from the same run. A slang plural is expanded before the check: `do you sell refurbs?` → `We also sell refurbished commuter bikes with a 6 month warranty. [1]`. Two facts that are on the pages but not in one sentence are a partial, never a stitched full answer: `do you do refurbs on sunday` → `The site doesn't cover that in one place. Here's what it says about hours: Sunday: closed [1] We also sell refurbished commuter bikes with a 6 month warranty. [2]`. The same holds for `do you sell refurbished e-bikes?`: the pages say they sell refurbished commuter bikes and repair electric bikes, and no sentence says they sell e-bikes. A number is checked, not ignored (1.0.11). A different number of the same kind is a correction: `is the full service $35?` → `The site says $140, not $35: The full service costs $140 and adds a drivetrain strip-down, wheel truing and a bearing check. [1]`, and `is puncture repair $35?` → `The site says $12 or $18, not $35: ...`. A number no sentence gives is named in a partial. Clock times are normalised, so `is the shop open 9 to 2 on saturday?` is full: 2 is 14:00 on the Saturday line. A missing word with no intent is named when its subject is covered: `do you do puncture repairs on weekends?` → `The site doesn't mention weekends. Here's what it says: A puncture repair costs $12 per wheel, or $18 with a new inner tube fitted. [1]`. A multi-part question is answered clause by clause: `what does a puncture cost and can u fix it while i wait` → `A puncture repair costs $12 per wheel, or $18 with a new inner tube fitted. [1]`, then `The site doesn't mention wait. Here's what it says about how long it takes: Most repairs are finished in two working days. [2]`.

Rules behind the table.

- **Required words** are the content words of the question. Not required: function words and light verbs (`do`, `can`, `while`, `until`, `offer`, `provide`, `available` ...), chat filler, the trade word `bike`. Each required word must be in the sentence, or be a synonym or an inflection of a word in it. Slang is expanded from a closed list (`refurbs`, `ebikes`, `e-bike`, `pics`, `info`, `asap`, `wot` and the rest in `sitebot/normalize.py`), and the expansion is checked like any typed word: `info` becomes "information", which must be on the pages.
- **Intent words** (`cost`, `open`, `take`, `warranty`, `book` ...) are covered only by a sentence that matches the intent (a price, an address, a duration). Payment nouns (`cheque`, `card`, `cash`, `credit`) must appear as themselves: "We accept cards" does not cover "cheque".
- **One sentence.** A full answer needs one sentence that covers every required word. Two facts are never stitched from two sentences, even when both are on the pages.
- **Main subject.** The subject nouns of the question, leaving out qualifiers (`weekend`, `free`, `lifetime`, `home`, `today`, day names, `same-day`). With no intent, every main subject must be on the pages, else the answer is refused: `do u sell tires for cars` is refused, because cars are not on the pages. With an intent, one covered main subject is enough for a partial (`how much does a car tune-up cost?` names the car and quotes the tune-up price). When no main subject is on the pages, the answer is a soft miss: `how much is xyzzy?` gets the two pages, never a price for something else.
- **Signal nouns** (`paypal` says payment, `whatsapp` says phone) are named as missing when the intent is answered.
- **Quoting.** A partial answer quotes the fewest sentences that cover the most required words (at most two, plus one that answers the intent) and never sends the question to the model. A full answer is phrased by the model over its one sentence. If the model declines, the answer is a soft miss.
- **Missing intent answers.** When a question's intent is not answered by any retrieved chunk, the chunks of the whole site that match the intent are added before the check.

**The keyless mock is a demo.** It picks the best matching sentences from the pages, so its wording is plain. With `--llm groq`, `--llm gemini` or Ollama, the model phrases the one-sentence full answers better, and the same rules decide which of the four kinds it is. Partial answers are the page's own sentences, quoted, with the disclosure written by the code.

## How well does it work? Measured, all sets

Fixture site (a made-up bike shop), run with `python -m sitebot.evalset` (version 1.0.13). Two embedders, because they give different numbers: hashed embeddings (built in; the default for `sitebot demo` and the eval) and bge-small (fastembed; downloads a model). Partial answers count as correct for in-scope questions only when the question has an uncovered term, and a partial always names one. For near-topic questions the test is that no near-topic question gets a full answer, and the combined-claim set below expects none.

| Set                                                                                           | Questions                 | Hashed embeddings      | bge-small (fastembed)  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Clean held-out, answerable answered                                                           | 15                        | 15 of 15               | 15 of 15               |
| Clean held-out, near-topic refused (rest disclosed)                                           | 10                        | 10 of 10 (0 disclosed) | 10 of 10 (0 disclosed) |
| Clean held-out, off-topic refused                                                             | 5                         | 5 of 5                 | 5 of 5                 |
| Slang and typos, answerable answered                                                          | 15                        | 15 of 15               | 15 of 15               |
| Slang and typos, should-refuse refused                                                        | 5                         | 5 of 5                 | 5 of 5                 |
| Slang, fresh, answerable answered                                                             | 8                         | 8 of 8                 | 8 of 8                 |
| Slang, fresh, should-refuse refused                                                           | 2                         | 2 of 2                 | 2 of 2                 |
| Multi-part (two questions in one), every clause right                                         | 10 questions (20 clauses) | 10 of 10 (20 of 20)    | 10 of 10 (20 of 20)    |
| Coverage: answer kind right (full / partial / soft / refuse), incl. 1.0.9 and 1.0.10 blockers | 41                        | 41 of 41               | 41 of 41               |
| Combined-claim: 15 questions stitched from words on the pages, answered as FULL               | 15                        | 0 full (15 partial)    | 0 full (15 partial)    |
| Number claims (1.0.12): 6 right numbers and currencies answered in full, 7 wrong ones (other amounts or other currencies) answered as a correction | 13 | 13 of 13 | not run |
| Typo-neighbour off-topic nouns (drones, mopeds, carts ... and 25 generated), refused          | 40                        | 40 of 40               | 40 of 40               |
| Tuned set, in-scope answered (all full)                                                       | 12                        | 12 of 12               | 12 of 12               |
| Tuned set, near-topic refused (rest disclosed)                                                | 10                        | 9 of 10 (1 disclosed)  | 9 of 10 (1 disclosed)  |
| Tuned set, off-topic refused                                                                  | 5                         | 5 of 5                 | 5 of 5                 |

Near-topic questions are refused or soft misses, except where the subject's covered part is on the pages: "How much does a car tune-up cost?" is a disclosed partial that names the car and quotes the bike tune-up price. None of the near-topic questions in the tuned, held-out and combined sets gets a full answer. If that disclosure is too loose for your site, delete the partial branch in `sitebot/chat.py` and it becomes a soft miss.

**Multi-part** questions are split into at most three clauses; each is answered, partly answered or named ("I don't know about ...") on its own. **The typo-neighbour** set exists because an earlier automatic typo repair rewrote off-topic nouns into the site's vocabulary (drones into "drop", carts into "card") and so passed the refusal check; repair only moves a mistyped word toward a small closed list of question words, never toward the site's own words, and that set must stay at 100 percent. **The combined-claim** set exists because a coverage check over the union of retrieved chunks gave full answers to stitched claims ("is the puncture repair warranty 6 months?" was full, from a refurbished-bikes warranty sentence and a puncture-price sentence); it must stay at 0 full.

**These numbers flatter the system.** Every set was used to fix a miss, so none is fully held out. The 1.0.8 coverage set and the slang examples from the review were written while the partial and soft-miss logic was being built. The 1.0.9 and 1.0.10 blocker rows came from a reviewer's failing questions and were added after the fixes, so they are not held out either. The combined-claim set was written from the fixture's own sentences after the one-sentence rule was built. The clean held-out set first scored 13 of 15 before the intent layer. It is also one small site; run `python -m sitebot.evalset` and write questions for your own site before promising anyone an accuracy. Details and every first-run number: [docs/EVAL.md](docs/EVAL.md).

## Quickstart for a real site

```bash
pip install -e .[embed]                               # optional: neural embeddings (bge-small via fastembed)
sitebot crawl https://example-prospect.com --max-pages 50
sitebot index example-prospect.com
export GROQ_API_KEY=...            # or GEMINI_API_KEY, or run Ollama; none = offline mock
sitebot serve example-prospect.com --port 8765     # open http://127.0.0.1:8765/demo
```

### On a prospect call: `sitebot share`

`/demo` works as is because it is the same origin as the API. **On any other page (a staging page, the prospect's real site) the widget only works if that page's origin is allowed with `--allow-origin`; otherwise it shows "Can't reach the assistant".** `sitebot share` prints the exact steps, or runs cloudflared for you:

```bash
sitebot share example-prospect.com --origin https://staging.example-prospect.com
```

By hand, with cloudflared (use `--trust-proxy xff-last` for ngrok or tailscale; never trust a header your tunnel does not set):

```bash
sitebot serve example-prospect.com --port 8765 \
  --allow-origin https://example-prospect.com --allow-origin https://www.example-prospect.com --trust-proxy cf
cloudflared tunnel --url http://127.0.0.1:8765
```

```html
<script src="https://YOUR-TUNNEL-HOST/widget.js" async></script>
```

```bash
sitebot pitch example-prospect.com --agency "Your Agency" --widget-url https://YOUR-TUNNEL-HOST
```

`pitch.html` is a one-pager with 5 sample Q&As built from the site's own headings and FAQ entries and run through the real pipeline; only fully covered answers appear (no refusals, no partial answers), samples are labelled, and it warns when the widget URL is localhost. If you change the site, `sitebot crawl` then `sitebot index` again: `serve` warns when the pages are newer than the index. With `--llm groq` or `--llm gemini`, visitor questions and page text go to that provider: see [docs/SAFETY.md](docs/SAFETY.md).

## Commands

| Command                               | What it does                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sitebot crawl URL --max-pages 50`    | Same-origin crawl that honors robots.txt (RFC 9309), writes `data/<site>/pages/*.md`. Also `--delay`, `--max-seconds`, `--max-crawl-delay`, `--crawler crawl4ai`, `--allow-private` |
| `sitebot index SITE`                  | Chunk, embed, store. `--embedder` is auto, fastembed or hash; `--max-chunks` caps memory                                                                                            |
| `sitebot serve SITE --port 8765`      | `/chat`, `/widget.js`, `/demo`. Also `--llm`, `--allow-origin`, `--trust-proxy MODE`, `--proxy-cidr`, `--rate-limit`, `--global-per-min`, `--global-per-day`, `--enable-lead`       |
| `sitebot share SITE`                  | Prospect-call setup: tunnel, CORS allow-list, safe rate-limit keying                                                                                                                |
| `sitebot ask SITE "question"`         | One question from the terminal                                                                                                                                                      |
| `sitebot pitch SITE --widget-url URL` | `pitch.html` with 5 sample Q&As                                                                                                                                                     |
| `sitebot demo`                        | End-to-end run on `examples/demo-site/` on a free port                                                                                                                              |

`--llm` is auto, mock, ollama, groq or gemini; `auto` prefers Ollama, then Groq, then Gemini, then the offline mock. For Ollama it uses `SITEBOT_OLLAMA_MODEL` or the smallest general chat model installed, and prints which. Embeddings are bge-small when fastembed is installed and a built-in hashed TF-IDF otherwise.

## For you if

- You sell chatbots to small businesses and want a demo on their content before the call ends.
- You run a small site and want a free assistant that does not invent answers.
- You want the data and the index on your own disk.

Not for you if you need multi-turn memory, JavaScript-heavy site coverage out of the box, or a hosted SaaS.
