## 🌟 Introduction
Welcome to the official repository for our survey paper, **"A Survey on Deep Image Restoration: Taxonomy, Evaluation and Future Trends"**. This repository serves as a comprehensive and continuously updated resource for the image restoration (IR) community, aiming to provide a holistic reference for researchers.

Image restoration is a fundamental problem in computer vision, aiming to recover high-quality images from degraded observations. The advent of deep learning has revolutionized this field, with a rapidly growing number of publications in top conferences. While several surveys have explored specific IR tasks or architectures, this work provides a unified and comprehensive review of over **1,500** papers, covering a wide range of restoration tasks, diverse architectures (CNNs, GANs, Transformers, Diffusion Models, and Mamba-based models), learning paradigms, datasets, and evaluation protocols.

![Milestones in Image Restoration Techniques](assets/images/technique_milestones.png)

This survey presents a comprehensive review of recent progress in deep learning-based image restoration. Our key contributions are:

1.  **A Novel Taxonomy:** We introduce a hierarchical taxonomy to structure the vast field of deep image restoration, categorizing methods by task, architecture, and learning strategy.
2.  **Comprehensive Literature Review:** We systematically review a wide range of restoration tasks, including denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, dehazing, deraining, desnowing, low-light enhancement, and unified restoration.
3.  **Analysis of Technical Evolution:** We trace the development of core technologies, from early CNNs to modern Transformers, Diffusion Models, and State-Space Models, analyzing their architectural foundations, network structures, and learning strategies.
4.  **Extensive Benchmarking:** We provide a comparative analysis of representative models on standard datasets to offer a clear performance landscape and facilitate fair benchmarking.
5.  **Future Directions:** We identify key challenges and highlight promising future research avenues to guide and inspire innovation in the IR community.

<h2 id="challenges">8. Open Challenges & Future Directions</h2>

Our survey identifies several key challenges and promising future directions in deep image restoration:

- **Algorithmic Challenges**:
  - **Mixed/Compound Degradations**: Most existing solutions are designed for single degradations. Effectively tackling multiple, commingled degradations in real-world scenarios remains an open challenge.
  - **Theoretical Understanding**: The theoretical underpinnings of deep architectures (e.g., U-Nets, Transformers) are still not well understood. A stronger theoretical foundation could guide more principled and efficient model designs.
  - **Training Stability**: Ensuring stable, reproducible training remains difficult, especially for complex generative models like GANs, which can suffer from mode collapse and hyperparameter sensitivity.

- **Generalization and Real-World Applicability**:
  - **Domain Gap**: A significant performance gap exists between models trained on synthetic data and their application to authentic, real-world images with complex and unknown degradations.
  - **Adversarial Robustness**: Enhancing model resilience to adversarial attacks is crucial for ensuring reliability in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical imaging.

- **Learning under Data Scarcity**:
  - **Few-Shot & Zero-Shot Learning**: Developing models that can adapt to new restoration tasks and unseen degradation types with minimal or no paired examples is a key research direction.
  - **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Improving performance when transferring models to novel domains (e.g., medical, satellite, artistic) with different data distributions remains a major challenge.

- **Computational Efficiency**:
  - **Lightweight Architectures**: Designing lightweight yet powerful models is essential for real-time processing and deployment on resource-constrained devices like mobile phones.
  - **Ultra-High-Definition Processing**: Developing efficient architectures that can handle UHD (4K/8K) images without prohibitive computational costs and memory overhead is crucial for practical applications.

- **Dataset Diversity**:
  - **Comprehensive Datasets**: There is a need for more diverse and representative datasets that cover a wider range of degradations, real-world conditions, and scenes.
  - **Bias Mitigation**: Addressing geographical, cultural, and other biases present in existing datasets is essential to build more equitable and globally effective restoration systems.

- **Explainability and Interpretability**:
  - **Trustworthy AI**: Building transparent models whose decision-making processes can be understood and trusted is critical, particularly in sensitive fields like forensics and medicine.
  - **Human-in-the-Loop**: Designing systems that can incorporate expert feedback is needed to improve interpretability, build user trust, and enhance performance.

- **User Interaction and Customization**:
  - **Interactive Restoration**: Moving beyond fully automatic systems to allow user guidance, such as adjustable parameters or region-of-interest selection, to achieve personalized results.
  - **Intelligent Agents**: Integrating intelligent agents and large language models (LLMs) to interpret high-level user instructions for customized and controllable restoration.

- **Multimodal and Context-Aware Restoration**:
  - **Leveraging Auxiliary Data**: Effectively using information from other modalities (e.g., text, depth, audio) to disambiguate and improve restoration quality.
  - **Semantic Awareness**: Building models that understand scene context to produce more plausible and coherent reconstructions, moving beyond pixel-level accuracy.

- **Benchmarking and Reproducibility**:
  - **Standardized Evaluation**: Establishing standardized benchmarks with real-world test data and transparent evaluation protocols is necessary for fair and meaningful comparison of methods.
  - **Open Science**: Promoting the release of code, models, and detailed experimental setups to improve reproducibility and accelerate scientific progress in the community.
