## The thread stays with you

You have probably done this. You spend an evening working something through with an AI: a project, a book, a decision. A week later you come back and it's a blank page. Or a better model comes out, and everything you told the old one stays behind in someone else's account.

PCS starts from a simple idea: **your continuity should belong to you, not to the model.**

It's a free Windows app that sits between you and the AI. Your conversations, the memories drawn from them and your notes live in an encrypted vault on your own PC. The AI is the visitor: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Llama, a model on OpenRouter, or one running on your own graphics card. Change it whenever you like. What you've built stays.

> On a Tuesday evening you talk with Claude about getting into astrophotography, and you settle on a first step: one familiar constellation, photographed with the camera you already own. You keep a stopping point and close PCS.
>
> Two weeks later you open PCS with a model running on your own PC. **Where you left off** is waiting: the stopping point, the memory behind it and an editable draft to pick the thread back up. You decide whether to send it.

**You stay in charge of what it remembers.** Every memory PCS writes links back to the words it came from, so you can read it, correct it in plain language or forget it. Saving a conversation and turning it into memories are separate choices, and a cloud model sees only what you allow it to use. AI-written memories can still miss or misread things, which is why every one of them is open to you.

Around that, PCS gives you a **Continuity Map** of how your saved subjects connect, **reading, notebooks, Practice and a Calendar** that work without an AI conversation, and optional **voice** and **screen sharing**.

## Quick start

1. Download **[PCS-0.9.181-Windows-x64.zip](https://github.com/PCS-PersonalContinuitySystem/PCS/releases/download/v0.9.181/PCS-0.9.181-Windows-x64.zip)** (42 MB) and extract the whole ZIP into a fresh writable folder.
2. Open **Start PCS.exe**, choose **Get started** and follow Quick setup. Keep your vault passphrase safe: PCS cannot recover it.
3. Choose **Get acquainted →** for a first conversation that PCS begins, or type a message and choose **Start & send**.

Use **Quit PCS** when you finish; closing the browser tab alone can leave PCS running. The **[setup guide](https://pcs-personalcontinuitysystem.github.io/PCS-site/getting-started.html)** covers each connection, voice, screen sharing and what to do when you're stuck.

**Upgrading?** Make an encrypted backup and quit PCS first, then follow **[Updating and restoring](https://pcs-personalcontinuitysystem.github.io/PCS-site/getting-started.html#upgrade)**. The **[release notes](https://github.com/PCS-PersonalContinuitySystem/PCS/releases/tag/v0.9.181)** list what changed and anything to check when coming from an older version.

**Linux or Mac?** Older experimental builds are on the [0.9.114 release page](https://github.com/PCS-PersonalContinuitySystem/PCS/releases/tag/v0.9.114). They are archived experiments and lack the current Windows features.

## Good to know

- PCS has no subscription or paid tiers. Provider API charges, and any subscription you use for ChatGPT sign-in, are separate. Local models and speech packs are separate downloads.
- Your saved records live in an encrypted vault on your PC. Cloud connections receive what their sharing controls allow; see **[Data and privacy](https://pcs-personalcontinuitysystem.github.io/PCS-site/data-and-privacy.html)**.
- AI replies, memories, transcription and research can be wrong. Each release's notes say what was tested and what was not.
