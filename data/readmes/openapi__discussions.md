The community space for **[@Openapi](https://github.com/openapi)** — APIs, open
source, ideas, projects and conversations.

## 👋 Welcome

Our repositories are where the code lives. This is where the people are.

Every Openapi SDK, tool and integration has its own repository, its own issues
and its own release notes. That works well for bugs and pull requests, and
badly for everything else: the questions that do not have a single right
answer, the thing you built over a weekend, the integration that should exist
and does not, the opinion you have about rate limits that nobody asked for.

All of that belongs here. You do not need to be a customer, a contributor, or
an expert. If you build things that talk to APIs, you are in the right place.

## 🚀 Join the conversation

### **[→ Open the Discussions](https://github.com/orgs/openapi/discussions)**

No introduction required. Pick a thread that looks interesting and say what you
think — a one-line answer is a real answer, and an upvote is a real
contribution.

## 💓 Openapi Pulse

The card at the top of this page is the **[Openapi Pulse](https://github.com/openapi/pulse)** —
a regular edition of what is moving in the ecosystem: an API worth a look, a
developer and a contributor from the community, and the discussion worth
joining this time around.

It runs on a rotation, so the people it features change on their own. Two ways
to end up on it:

* be a member of the [Openapi organization](https://github.com/orgs/openapi/people)
* add yourself to the [contributors registry](https://github.com/openapi/contributors) —
  it is a single pull request, and it is what puts you in the rotation

Every edition is archived in the
[Openapi Pulse category](https://github.com/orgs/openapi/discussions/categories/openapi-pulse).

## 🤝 Ways to take part

* **Answer a thread.** One line is enough.
* **Show what you built.** Even the forty-line script nobody asked for.
* **Ask for an integration.** If several people ask for the same one, that is a roadmap.
* **Contribute to a repository.** Every Openapi project takes pull requests.
* **Bring a question from your own work.** The ones from real projects are the good ones.

Be decent to each other, assume the other person is trying to help, and keep it
technical. That is the whole moderation policy.
