- **[MaxIM: Maximally Informative Incremental Summarization via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12825)**  `arXiv:2611.12825`  
  _Jihwan Jeong, Guy Tennenholtz, Yinlam Chow, Chih-Wei Hsu, Craig Boutilier_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  AI systems must often maintain bounded memory of unbounded interaction histories. However, optimizing the memory representation, and its updates, for downstream task utility is difficult for standard reinforcement learning (RL) due to severe credit assignment bottlenecks induced by sparse, delayed rewards. We introduce MaxIM (Maximally Informative Incremental Memory), a framework that formulates incremental summarization as a capacity-constrained, information-theoretic reward-shaping problem. Grounded in value suboptimality bounds, we show that bounding sequential information loss motivates maximizing the predictive log-likelihood of future, task-relevant targets, a variational lower bound on predictive sufficiency. We introduce a recursive consistency condition to ensure summaries are grounded by maximizing mutual information (MI) with the observed history. We mitigate credit assignment issues with a parameterized asymmetric critic that computes step-wise variational MI bounds for dense reward shaping. Empirical evaluation on long-horizon scientific reading and multi-turn dialogue benchmarks shows that MaxIM outperforms RL, supervised fine-tuning, and prompt-focused baselines on factual grounding, comprehensive autorater metrics, and downstream task utility across strict token budgets and extended horizons, without the need for expensive human preference labels.

- **[LogCDM: Interpretable Log Anomaly Detection via Behavioral Graph Drift and Adaptive Signal Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12775)**  `arXiv:2611.12775`  
  _Suryansh Talukdar, Taha Sarwat, Jin Soung Yoo_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Log-based anomaly detection is increasingly dominated by large pretrained models, from BERT-scale encoders to 8B-parameter language models, which require GPUs at inference time and are difficult to deploy on the monitoring nodes where logs are produced. We present LogCDM, a compact detector that scores each log session using three complementary signals: the next-event surprise of a small causal Transformer, the distance between a graph-attention embedding of the session's own transition graph and that of a normal reference graph, and a transition-drift statistic that we show is exactly a frequency-weighted chi-squared divergence between transition kernels. The divergence view yields an asymptotic null distribution, a length-standardized score, and a per-edge decomposition that explains each alarm. With 8.1-8.8M parameters and no GPU required at inference, LogCDM reaches F1 scores of 0.984 on HDFS, 0.870 on chronologically split BGL, and 0.850 on Thunderbird, within 0.013-0.12 of an 8.1B-parameter LLM detector evaluated under the same protocol and above all compact baselines on BGL. It scores a session in 5.5-10.5 ms on a CPU. Ablations show that the structural signals add 0.025-0.030 F1 on BGL and Thunderbird but almost nothing on HDFS, consistent with the learned fusion weights.

  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
