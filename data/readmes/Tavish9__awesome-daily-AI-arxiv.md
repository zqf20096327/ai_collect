- **[Functional Depth Is Learned: Isolating the Computation That Layers Produce Only Together](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27911)**  `arXiv:2611.27911`  
  _JaeSeong Kim, KyungTae Lim_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Transformer performance scales with depth, but layer count merely counts the opportunities for sequential computation: layers could still behave as nearly independent refinements that barely depend on one another. We study how transformers develop functional depth during pretraining, introducing an exact inclusion-exclusion decomposition that isolates the connected component, i.e., the computation that appears only when a specified set of layers acts together under identity ablation. Tracking this quantity across public checkpoints of Pythia, OLMo, and Amber yields three findings. First, write-relative connected coordination strengthens from its initialization or earliest-checkpoint baseline as pretraining proceeds, across all measured interaction orders. Second, these connected components are behaviorally consequential under direct intervention: deleting them damages perplexity more than deleting a norm-matched random direction at the same site in all 35 measured model-order cells. Third, pairwise representation similarity is consistently negatively correlated with pairwise connected coordination across seven models, yet the two signals are not interchangeable: similarity- and coordination-based block-removal criteria often agree in Pythia but diverge on Amber. Together, these results suggest that architectural depth provides the opportunity for sequential computation, while pretraining progressively organizes cross-layer dependencies within that depth.
  </details>

- **[Speculative Decoding Is Less Speculative Than You Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27891)**  `arXiv:2611.27891`  
  _Guanning Zeng, Andrea Zanette_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Speculative decoding accelerates language-model inference by preparing candidate continuations before the target model generates them. Its efficiency depends on how well limited drafting computation anticipates the path the target will actually follow. Anticipating this path involves two sources of uncertainty: the target model's predictions and the randomness of sampling. Yet the former requires a target-model forward pass, while the latter can be fixed before drafting. Building on this observation, we introduce DICE (Draw-Informed Candidate Selection), which pre-samples and synchronizes verification randomness across drafting and verification. By combining these known draws with predictions from an existing drafter, DICE allocates computation toward continuations more likely to be used in the current sampling run. This principle guides node selection throughout draft-tree construction and extends to asynchronous prefetching, where DICE predicts which next-round drafts to prepare. Without additional drafter training or changes to the target sampling distribution, DICE achieves state-of-the-art decoding throughput across diverse settings, with speedups of up to 24.6pp over strong baselines.
